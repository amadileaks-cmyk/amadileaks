Meet the Teachers: Why We Keep Classes Small on Purpose

By the Art of Yoga teaching team

We get asked a version of this question a lot: "Why don't you add more spots to the popular classes?" It's a fair question — some of our evening sessions fill up fast, and we know that's frustrating when you're trying to plan your week. Here's the honest answer: we cap class sizes on purpose, and we're not planning to change that.

It's not about scarcity

A studio can always make more money by packing more mats into a room. We've chosen not to, because the thing that actually keeps people coming back — year after year, not just for a January resolution — is an instructor who can see you. Notice that your shoulder's been tight for three weeks. Notice you had a rough day before you even said anything. That kind of attention just isn't possible at 40 students to one teacher.

What "small" actually means here

Most of our classes run 12 to 18 students. Enough for a real room energy — you're not practicing alone — but small enough that every instructor on our team can give hands-on adjustments, call people by name, and actually remember what you told them about your knee last Tuesday.

"Our instructors don't teach poses at a room. They teach people, who happen to be practicing poses."

Who's teaching

Our teaching team is a mix of longtime Columbus instructors and newer teachers trained directly in our studio's style — a grounded, no-frills approach that leans more into breath and alignment than performance. Several of our instructors came to yoga themselves as total beginners, often through recovery from an injury or a hard season of life, which shapes how they teach: patiently, and without ego.

What this means for you

If a class you want is full, it's genuinely worth grabbing the next time slot or joining the waitlist — spots do open up, and we'd rather you wait a day for a seat in a room where the teacher actually knows your name than squeeze you into one where you're a number.
