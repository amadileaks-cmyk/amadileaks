New to the Studio? Here's What Your First Class Looks Like

By the Art of Yoga teaching team

Walking into a yoga studio for the first time is, for a lot of people, the single biggest reason they put it off for years. Not the poses — the walking in. What do I wear? Do I need to bring anything? What if everyone else already knows what they're doing? We get asked some version of this almost every week, so here's exactly what to expect, start to finish.

Before you arrive

Show up about 10 minutes early for your first visit. That's enough time to fill out a short intake card (just your name, contact info, and anything we should know — old injuries, pregnancy, anything like that), meet your instructor, and pick a spot in the room without feeling rushed.

Wear whatever you'd comfortably move in — leggings, joggers, a t-shirt. You do not need special "yoga clothes." The only real rule is nothing too loose that'll flip over your head during a forward fold.

What to bring

  • Nothing, if you don't own anything yet. We have loaner mats, blocks, and straps for first-timers, free of charge.
  • Water. We have a filling station, but bring a bottle if you have one.
  • An open mind about "beginner." Most of our classes are mixed-level, meaning lifelong practitioners and first-timers are often on mats next to each other. Nobody is watching you — they're focused on their own breath.

During class

Every class opens with a few minutes of just sitting and breathing before any movement starts. This is normal, not a slow start — it's how we get everyone out of "rushing from the car" mode. Your instructor will cue poses by name and describe them out loud, and in a beginner-friendly class they'll offer modifications for almost everything. If a pose doesn't feel right in your body that day, you're always welcome to rest in Child's Pose — every instructor will tell you this, every class, no questions asked.

"The only prerequisite for your first class is showing up. Everything else, we'll walk you through."

After class

Most people leave a little looser than they arrived and surprisingly calmer than they expected. Stick around for a few minutes if you want — our community tends to linger and chat after the later morning and evening classes especially.

One more thing

If you've been putting off your first class because you're worried about not being flexible enough — that's like avoiding the gym because you're not strong enough yet. Flexibility is the result of practice, not the requirement for starting it.

Your first class is on us

New students practice free the first time — just show up, we'll handle the rest.
