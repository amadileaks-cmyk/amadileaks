5 Stretches to Do Before You Even Get Out of Bed

By the Art of Yoga teaching team

If your feet hit the floor every morning already stiff, you're not alone — and you don't need a mat, a studio, or even ten free minutes to do something about it. This is the routine we hand to brand-new students in their first week, because it works before you've built any habit at all. Just your bed, your breath, and about two minutes.

Stiffness in the morning isn't really about your muscles "waking up slow." It's mostly about fluid — the cartilage in your joints rehydrates overnight, and the connective tissue around your spine and hips tightens up from hours of stillness. A few slow, deliberate movements before you're vertical gives all of that a head start, so the first steps of your day don't feel like the first steps after a long flight.

1. Full-Body Reach (30 seconds)

Lying flat on your back, stretch your arms overhead and your toes toward the foot of the bed at the same time, like you're trying to get taller. Hold for a slow count of five breaths, release, repeat twice. This re-lengthens the spine after a night of being curled up.

2. Knee-to-Chest Rock (30 seconds)

Hug both knees into your chest and rock gently side to side. This massages the lower back — the part of the body that takes the most quiet abuse from a mattress that's too soft or too firm.

3. Seated Spinal Twist (20 seconds each side)

Sit up, cross one leg over the other, and twist gently toward your back leg, one hand braced behind you. Twisting is one of the fastest ways to get circulation moving through the whole torso.

4. Neck Rolls (20 seconds)

Slow half-circles, chin to chest and around to each shoulder. Skip rolling the head all the way back — we keep this one gentle on purpose.

5. Seated Forward Fold, Feet on Floor (30 seconds)

Feet flat, hinge forward from the hips with a long spine, let your head hang heavy. This is the one students tell us they feel the most immediately — especially through tight hamstrings and the back of the neck.

"You don't have to earn the right to feel good in your body. Two minutes, still in bed, counts."

None of this replaces a full practice — but it's a real, honest way to start the day differently, and it's exactly the kind of small, doable thing we build our classes around. If five minutes in bed felt good, an hour on the mat with people cheering you on quietly from their own corner of the room feels even better.
